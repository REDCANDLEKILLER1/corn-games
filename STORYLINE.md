# Legend of Corn — Storyline & Theme
_Written 2026-11-17 at Michael's direction: build the plot from the game's real
material, Zelda-style. This is the script the in-game intro dialogues use._

## The theme
**The Sleeping Harvest** — the official story (rewritten 2026-11-18 at
Michael's direction from Zelda II: The Adventure of Link, corn-grown;
it supersedes the earlier storm-scattering telling). Long ago, the
Harvest King ruled a valley so rich the corn grew taller than the silos.
Hidden in the Great Barn he kept the **Golden Cob of Courage**, and only
his daughter, **Princess Poppy**, knew its resting place. The Storm
Wizard — a crow-hearted conjurer in the Blight King's service — demanded
the secret; Poppy would not tell, so he laid an endless sleep on her, and
she has not woken in a hundred harvests. Before he died, the old king
sealed the Great Barn with a binding force and set a shrine in eight
barns across the valley, each with an empty socket waiting for a
**crystallized corn cob**, each cob guarded by a keeper. When all eight
cobs are set and shining, the seal breaks and the Golden Cob can wake
the princess — and the crows are watching: if the hero falls, the Blight
King's followers will scatter his seed corn on their master's ashes and
raise him to wither every field forever. This spring Grandma Pearl found
the mark on a farm boy's hand — a single golden kernel, bright as
sunrise. His name is Corn Man. The final trial inside the Great Barn is
the Wizard's last trick: a double of Corn Man himself. And when Poppy
wakes, the story ends the way farm stories should — with a wedding.

Courage, in this telling, is not being unafraid. It is walking into the next
field anyway.

## The plot
1. **The seal.** The old Harvest King sealed the Great Barn and set a
   shrine with an empty socket in 8 barns across the valley. A crystallized
   corn cob waits in each barn's seed room, guarded by a keeper the
   Wizard's night woke up wrong: hens bold, machines angry, old things
   hungry.
2. **Empty hands.** The Country Stores are gone — the Wizard's night took them too.
   Everything Corn Man needs must be *found* where the Wizard's night scattered it. And
   the first one is an adventure, not a pickup: early morning fog lies over
   the fields, and Corn Man must fight his way north with his bare fists —
   four fields, past Middle Field — to the Pedestal Grove, where his Wooden
   Wrench waits thrust into the old farm stone. He takes hold with both
   hands, and PULLS. (Once the wrench is in his hand, the fog lifts — the
   sun is up.) Then a Lantern on the Sunny East Field, a Bow by the Creek
   & Pond, golden arrows in the West Meadow. Crows carry off arrows — take
   them back from crows.
3. **The eight barns.** Beat each keeper, take its heart, follow the path to
   the seed room, and claim the barn piece. His wrench grows with his heart:
   5 hearts earns the Silver Wrench, 10 the Golden.
4. **The Great Barn (Level 9).** With 8 pieces in place the barn rises — but
   the storm left SUPER CORN MAN inside: Corn Man's own shadow, hurtable
   only by his own weapons (the corn thrower and golden arrows).
5. **The wedding.** The barn stands, the farm is whole, and Corn Man marries
   the woman in the white dress.
6. **World 2.** The farm switches around in the night; every keeper is 25%
   stronger. Same wedding at the end — some things the storm can't move.

## The field creatures (storm-touched, all stronger than they look)
- **Chicken** — pecks, stalks, and lunges. 3 hits with a fist, 2 with the
  Wooden Wrench, 1 with anything better.
- **Pig** — grazes, paws the ground, and charges like a battering ram.
- **Crow** — circles, caws, and dives. Carries stolen arrows.
- **Goat** — zigzags in, lowers its head, and headbutts; dizzy afterward.
- **Scarecrow** — stands still as death, then lurches. Swipes at arm's reach.
- **Mole** — travels underground as a moving mound; pops up to swipe.
- **Tractor** — chugs, revs, and drives straight through anything.

## The damage law (Michael's algorithm)
Fist = 1 · Wooden Wrench = 2 · Silver Wrench = 3 · Golden Wrench = 4.
Spin attack = double the swing. So a chicken (3 HP) takes 3 fists, 2 wooden
hits, 1 silver hit — and from the silver upgrade on, it's one hit at any
level, forever. Everything else on the farm is tougher than a chicken.

## The map, the roads and the Pack (v7, Michael 2026-11-18)

- **The Farm Map is drawn, not gridded**: painted from the game's own data — real ground colors, terrain, fences with gaps where you can walk through, barns with roofs and level badges, the farmhouse, the pedestal, the silo, the pond, the maze, the graveyard — with a compass, like the Zelda 1 map poster.
- **Fog of war**: a field is charted only once Corn Man has stood on it. Unwalked fields are fog: no name, no barn, no secrets. World 2 fogs separately — it is a switched-around farm and must be learned fresh.
- **The map moves**: pinch or scroll to zoom (field names fade in as you zoom), drag to look around, tap a charted field to read about it — the note gives compass directions from where you stand.
- **Nobody wanders and hopes**: farmers stand on the crossroads fields and TALK — compass points, field counts, landmarks — on top of the Middle Field farmer who already knew every road.
- **PAUSE is the Pack** (the Zelda subscreen): the charted map, an inventory grid of everything he carries, the eight Great Barn pieces and his hearts — and **equip**: take the wrench off and the red button punches again; put it on and it swings; choose whether THROW throws kernels, bow arrows or gold arrows. The music keeps playing while the Pack is open, like the original.

## Corn Village & the houses (v8, Michael 2026-11-18)

- **Middle Field is Corn Village now**: dirt paths, a well in the square, and three full-size houses — Grandma Pearl's Cottage, Hank's Forge, Fern's Farmhouse — each with a door you walk through into a furnished room.
- **People live here**: Pearl and her granddaughter June, Blacksmith Hank, and Fern. They are drawn characters with names, and walking up to them opens a chat-bubble conversation. Pearl tells the storm story and gives corn for the road; Hank teaches the wrench lore (silver at five hearts, gold at ten, the spin) and gives tractor oil; June knows four secrets; Fern tells the wedding legend of the Great Barn.
- **Every talking farmer on the farm is a drawn character now** — the emoji stand-ins are gone.
- **The Level 4 hut is a full-size cottage** on its pond island. No more person-sized buildings.

## The houses, upgraded + the Hayloft Inn (v9, Michael 2026-11-18)

- Michael sent Minish Cap houses as the bar — **the houses were rebuilt to it**: a great scalloped thatch roof over plaster and stone, chimney smoke, shuttered windows, an arched door with a hanging sign; inside, thick stone walls, a lit fireplace, a bookcase of colorful books, real beds, and a keepsake on the north wall (Pearl's picture, Hank's shield and crossed tools, Fern's wreath).
- **The whimsy pass (v10):** Michael's verdict on v9 was "still squarey" — so the houses were redrawn from the reference itself: a rounded golden haystack of thatch with tufted scallop rows, a fringed hem, eyebrow dormers, a hooded porch over a round-arched door, and rounded stones under it all. The interiors softened with it — rounded rugs, arched firebox, arched doors.
- **The secret chime (v11):** the first of Michael's own generated sounds is in the game — a 16-bit secret-discovered chime that rings at every reveal: the leaning secrets (stairs, hatches, the coin cache), a Super Heart or the ladder appearing from a burned bale, and a coin stash glittering under a burned shed. An empty shed stays quiet — no secret, no chime.
- **The pull fanfare + the ceremony (v12):** Michael's second generated sound — the sword-pull / item-get fanfare — rings at every milestone get: puzzle pieces, the Overalls, Super Hearts, the ladder, the stash, the lantern, the bow, the gold quiver. And the wrench pull itself now plays the moment from the video he sent: the wrench held high in a turning radiance, gold sparkles bursting off the stone, the world holding still. (His file runs 13s with a quiet head — playback starts at the first note and fades: pickups ~3.5s, the pull ~6s.)
- **The night at the inn (v13):** Michael's third sound — the bed sound — turned sleeping into a timed scene, at his direction: step to a bed and the regular music stops, his sound plays, and the room blacks out in step with it (gone by 2.6s, full black till 5.0s). At 5.0s he wakes — hearts full, the day turned — and whatever music was playing before starts again **from the beginning**, while morning fades in over the sound's tail. The old tap-through sleep pages retired; the sound's own 7.6 seconds are the clock.
- **The village theme (v14):** Michael's fourth sound — a full Suno song — is Corn Village's own music. It loops while Corn Man is on the village field or inside any village house; step out of the village and the regular walk track resumes where it left off. Sleeping at Ruth's inn now hands the village song back at the wake, from its beginning, like any other.
- **Being led (v15, Michael 2026-11-18):** "you gotta be led, you can't just [drop him in]." **Eli** the greeter stands on Home Farm by the spawn with a five-page conversation that lays out the whole road: the wrench in the grove stone, four fields north, signs to follow, the village folks, then the barns east-first for the Lantern. **Wooden signs** stand along the north road (Home Farm ×2 — north and east, the field above home, Corn Village crossroads, the field below the grove, the grove gate): step up to one and it reads itself out in the chat bubble, directions and distances both. The drawn farm map now paints the north road and the village's four houses on charted fields. Same round, Michael's clutter cuts: the **map screen lost all its writing** (no hint paragraph, no legend wall — just the map, grown to 68vh, and the one readout line), and the **top HUD bar shows only on the game screen** — hearts and chips before the game starts were clutter over the title art; hearts live in the Pack too.
- **The pause menu is the Zelda subscreen (v16, Michael 2026-11-18):** rebuilt to his A Link to the Past reference, pretty much exactly — **ITEM** grid (tap to equip) with **DO** under it on the left (TALK / READ / PULL / PUSH / SWING-or-PUNCH / SPIN / THROW / BURN / CROSS WATER, greyed until he can do them); on the right, **IN HIS HANDS** (the reference's selected-item box), the **BARN PIECES** panel, and **EQUIPMENT** with his hearts. His question — "do we have anything similar to a crystal?" — answered in the build: **the barn pieces ARE our crystals** (collect all 8, the endgame opens), so they sit in the crystal panel as eight diamond slots that fill gold as they come home. The mini-map panel left the Pack (the full map is one tap away) and the note paragraphs went with the rest of the writing.
- **No flying texts (v17, Michael 2026-11-18):** the house-name banners painted across the village houses and the name labels over people's heads are gone. Words live in the UI — the banner names a house when you walk in, the chat bubble carries every conversation — and the world itself stays clean.
- **The Sleeping Harvest is the story now (v18, Michael 2026-11-18):** the opening pages are Grandma Pearl telling it over Corn Man's marked hand; the eight collectibles are **crystallized corn cobs** with their own names (Golden Dawn, Amber, Moonshadow, Orchard, Rain Pearl, Storm, Ember, Harvest), drawn as real crystal cobs on their seed-room pedestals; the pause menu's diamonds became cobs in sockets under a CRYSTAL COBS panel; and the old Barn Puzzle is **the Great Barn Seal** — every cob set breaks 1/8 of the seal and the Great Barn shines through.
- **Michael's playtest rules (v19, 2026-11-18).** Throwing is a SKILL, not a birthright: Corn Man cannot throw anything until Grandma Pearl's gift — her **Kernel Pouch** — teaches his throwing arm (the THROW button hides until then; Eli points the way). Enemies drop hearts the Zelda way (chickens 35%, the rest 22%, power hearts rarer). **Hay bales answer to a blade only**: the wrench or Corn Sword cuts plain hay (corn or a heart may shake loose); fists never cut, and the lantern's special bales and the secret bale never cut. **Secrets are earned**: the maze stairs are found by standing on the hidden spot; the tombstone, the lone bale and the village cache rock must be PUSHED (hold into them) — the cache loot appears at the rock. And the village dirt paths are scenery in the step loop, which un-hides Fern's Farmhouse door and the crossroads sign.
- **House texture pass (v20, 2026-11-18).** Michael: the houses weren't textured enough — "the original Zelda was just better." Researched the ALttP tileset craft and applied it: the v10 haystack silhouette stays, but stone is now individual stones over mortar (lit top edge, shadowed bottom, foundation course), the thatch has tuft rows with shade pooled under each row, straw strokes down the slope and a quiet ordered speckle, doors are planks with grain running along the wood plus iron bands and studs, windows glow warm, and interiors got mortar courses and plank grain. All texture is position-hashed — identical every frame, detail without flicker.
- **The Hayloft Inn** is the village's fourth house. Innkeeper Ruth keeps it. Step up to a bed and Corn Man sleeps: good night, then morning — **every heart full, and the farm's day counter turns**. Hurt all you like out there; the inn makes it right.

## The dialogue script (as shipped in the game)
Zelda rules: short lines, plain words, one idea per page, tap NEXT to read
on. The full text lives in the game file as `INTRO_TEXT` — opening, one
intro per level (played on first entry: Corn Man walks in, then the words
come), and a World 2 opening. Level intros are story, not instructions —
the keeper's own hint banner still fires at the boss door.
